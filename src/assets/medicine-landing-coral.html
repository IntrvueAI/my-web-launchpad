What actually happens in an MMI

Most UK medical schools run a Multiple Mini Interview — not one long conversation, but a circuit of short stations, each testing something different. You get a minute (or nothing at all, depending on the school) to read what's coming, then the clock starts. One station might be an ethical scenario with no right answer. The next might put an actual person in front of you — an anxious patient, a friend who's just told you something they shouldn't have — and how they react depends entirely on what you do. Then the bell goes, whether you've finished your sentence or not, and you move to the next room.

The bit almost nobody tells you: the format is different at every school. Manchester gives you zero reading time anywhere in the circuit. Leeds gives you two minutes before every station. Some schools don't run stations at all — Glasgow is a single 30-minute panel. A question bank doesn't prepare you for any of that. A timed, adaptive one does.

A character, not a question card

Roleplay stations put a real person opposite you — with a mood that changes based on what you say, and information that only comes out if you ask the right way. Reassure them falsely and they get more anxious, not less. Nothing about that can be memorised.

Timed the way your actual school times it

We checked Leeds's and Manchester's own admissions pages directly — not a coaching site's guess. Leeds: 8 stations, reading time before each. Manchester: 5 stations, zero prep, cold start. Practise against the real clock, not an untimed chat.

Current affairs that expires on purpose

Health policy moves fast — a government changes, a bill passes, a strike ends. Most "hot topics" lists are written once and go stale. Ours has real expiry dates: a station gets pulled from circulation the moment its facts might mislead you, instead of quietly staying wrong.

What this isn't

  • We don't score eye contact, warmth, or tone — only what's actually said. If a real interviewer would judge your body language, we're honest that we can't.
  • We won't tell you the "correct answer" to an ethics station — there isn't one, and any product that gives you one is teaching you to fail the actual interview.
  • We will never tell you your odds of getting an offer. Nobody has that data, and providers who claim to are making it up.
  • This is early access. Content is being added and reviewed continuously — some stations are still awaiting a clinician's sign-off before we'd call them finished.
